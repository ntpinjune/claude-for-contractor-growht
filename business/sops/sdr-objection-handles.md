# SDR Objection Handles — Real Handles for Real Objections

Built from: Noah's framing + Jack M (Impression Empire) + Matt Ryder sales course.
Companion to: [sdr-call-rubric.md](sdr-call-rubric.md) and the CG Setter/Closing Script.

**How to use this:**
1. SDRs read top to bottom before their first shift of the week. Understand the LOGIC, not just the words.
2. Pick ONE objection and drill the handle 20-30 times out loud until it's muscle memory.
3. In-call: follow the framework, don't try to improvise from memory. Parrot → Clarify → Guard drop → Isolate → Handle → Question-close.

**The 4 objections that killed every deal in Jowanna's 2026-04-23 batch** — and the exact handle for each.

## 1. "What if I don't close the deal? Am I paying for leads that ghost me?"

**What happened on Call 5:** Prospect said "if I get a lead, I have to pay you for the lead before I even contacted, you know, the potential customer or even if I, you know, before I close the deal." Jowanna rambled about trust, reputation, five years of experience. Prospect said "I'll think about it" and hung up.

**Why this objection comes up:** It's a fair concern. The prospect is pre-qualifying the risk. They're not rejecting — they're asking for the math to work.

### The handle (Noah's framing + question-based close)

1. **Parrot + clarify:** *"Totally — when you say 'what if I don't close,' help me understand: what's your current close rate when you meet with a qualified prospect?"*
   - Listen. Write down the number. This is the ROI data point you need.

2. **Guard drop:** *"Yeah, got it. Don't even worry about it — most people ask this, it's the right question."*

3. **Isolate:** *"So apart from the 'what if I don't close' piece, is there anything else that would stop us moving forward?"*

4. **Handle with the math:**
   > *"Let's just do the math real quick. You close around [40%] of qualified prospects. That means for every 10 leads I send you, you close 4. At $150 per lead, you're paying $1,500 to close 4 jobs. What's your average job size? [$15,000]. That's $60,000 in revenue from a $1,500 spend. Even if you had a terrible week and only closed 1 out of 10, that's $15,000 from a $1,500 spend. How's the math looking?"*

5. **Question-close:** *"With all of that in mind, what do you think is the most intelligent decision from here?"*

### Why this handle works

- The "ask their close rate first" move (Noah's insight) is key. It engages the prospect in answering a question about their own business — they can't dismiss their own number.
- It exposes that their fear is irrational as long as they can close at ANY reasonable rate. If they say their close rate is 80%, the math is insane. If they say 10%, you have a different conversation (maybe they're not actually qualified to close with our leads).
- Matt Ryder: *"Don't argue with their fear — use logic to walk them into reality."*
- Jack: *"How could I guarantee an ROI from all the way over here? If you were me, how would you do that?"* — the logic kills the objection.

### What NOT to say

- ❌ "Don't worry, our leads are high quality." (Claim they can't verify.)
- ❌ "We have 5 years of experience." (Irrelevant — doesn't change their risk.)
- ❌ "Trust us, you'll close them." (Authority-reducing.)

## 2. "Can we just try one job first?" / "Let's see if it works before I commit"

**What happened on Call 3:** Bay Area concrete contractor — strong KYC, $100k average job sizes, clearly a fit. He said *"Can we wait until you see if one job works? Let's try one job and see if it works. If it works for me and you because eventually you got to make money and I got to make money, right?"*

**This is actually a BUYING signal disguised as an objection.** The prospect is ready to say yes — they just want smaller commitment. Jowanna missed it and restated the price. He walked.

### The handle — Noah's new offer (use this as soon as it's built)

Once the "1 free appointment with setup" offer is live (see todos), this becomes the close:

> *"I love that you're thinking this way — it actually lines up with how we're set up. When you pay the setup today, your first qualified appointment is included free. So you literally get to see if it works on the first one. Zero risk on the lead fee. What time works for me to send you the payment link — now or end of day?"*

That's an **assumptive close** + **risk reversal** in one move. The prospect asked for a trial; you gave them one — but anchored to payment TODAY.

### The handle if you don't have the free-first-appointment offer yet

1. **Parrot:** *"Try one job first — gotcha. When you say 'one job,' what does that actually look like to you? Paying only if you close it, or something else?"*

2. **Guard drop:** *"Don't even worry about it. Most serious operators ask for some version of this."*

3. **Isolate:** *"If we could figure out a way to lower your risk on the first one, is there anything else stopping us?"*

4. **Handle with reframe:**
   > *"Here's the thing — we're already doing that. The $150 lead fee only happens when we've qualified the lead and booked the appointment. You're not paying for a 'maybe.' You're paying for a qualified prospect showing up. The setup fee gets the whole acquisition system running — that's what covers the first month of ads, the qualification system, the SEO. Without that, there's nothing to 'try.' But I hear you — want to know what my best clients do? They treat the first 10 appointments as the sample. If 4 of them close, we're good. If fewer than 2 close, we're doing something wrong and I fix it personally. Fair?"*

### Why this handle works

- You reframe "try one job" as "try the first 10 appointments" — which is what you actually want anyway. You flip THEIR frame into YOUR frame.
- You make yourself accountable ("I fix it personally") — builds trust without reducing authority.
- Matt Ryder: *"When someone pushes back, they're often giving you a buying condition. Listen for it."*

## 3. "I want a % of signed contract instead — no ad spend, no setup fee"

**What happened on Call 4:** Sales-sophisticated prospect (Alex). Said *"I don't want to pay ad fee, ad spend. I want just flat contract commission. 2-2.5% of signed contracts. $100,000 project, you get $2,500."* Jowanna couldn't reframe — said *"I'll talk to my partners"* — authority collapse. Call lost.

### The handle — Noah's math-based reframe

1. **Parrot:** *"You want commission only — 2-2.5% of signed contracts. Got it. Why that specifically? Have you done that with other vendors?"*
   - Let him vent. Write it down.

2. **Guard drop:** *"I actually get it — it sounds fairer on the surface."*

3. **Isolate:** *"If I could show you why the flat model protects you better, is there anything else stopping us?"*

4. **Handle with the math:**
   > *"Here's why I think the flat-fee model is actually better for YOU — not us. With a commission model, you're paying the same % on every job regardless of complexity. A $30,000 bathroom remodel and a $300,000 custom home pay us proportionally — $750 on the small one, $7,500 on the big one. But the work to book those appointments is identical on our end. Which means on big jobs, you're OVERPAYING. And on small jobs, you're paying us for something we haven't earned."*
   >
   > *"With flat $150 per qualified booked appointment, it doesn't matter if the job is $30k or $300k — your cost of acquisition is the same tiny percentage either way. On a $300k job, $150 is 0.05%. That's insane."*
   >
   > *"We're not optimizing for your average job — we're optimizing for your biggest jobs. Because that's where your margin is. Does that make sense?"*

5. **Question-close:** *"If the math works — and I think you can see it does — what do you think is the most intelligent decision from here?"*

### Why this handle works

- You flip the objection on its head. He thinks commission is fairer — but you prove it actually robs him on big deals (his highest-margin work).
- You don't argue the model — you argue his economic interest. That's how Matt Ryder teaches logic-based handles.
- You DON'T go back to your partners. You're the owner. You reframe on-call. (Jack: *"From business owner to business owner, here's what I'd do..."*)

### What NOT to say

- ❌ *"I'll talk to my partners."* (This is what killed Call 4. Never say it.)
- ❌ *"That's not how we work."* (Defensive.)
- ❌ *"Well, other clients prefer..."* (Deflects instead of answering.)

## 4. "I've been burned before" / "Your pitch sounds the same as the last guys"

**What happened on Call 2 and Call 4:** Prospects said some version of *"I just came from those guys and those guys told me the same thing that you're telling me."* Jowanna explained more differentiation. Prospects disengaged.

**Why this objection comes up:** It's not really about you — it's about their past pain. They need to feel heard before they can buy from you. If you jump to differentiation, you skip the emotional step they need.

### The handle — Matt Ryder's reframe (most powerful)

1. **STOP and reframe with respect:**
   > *"Hold on — you've tried this before? [pause] That actually tells me something really important. It tells me you're someone who doesn't give up, someone who doesn't quit. The fact that you're even on this call after it didn't work out before means you're in the top 1-2-3% of operators who actually want to find a real solution. Most people get burned and just stop trying. Well done."*

2. **Isolate + get the real story:** *"Walk me through what happened. What specifically didn't work?"*
   - Let them vent. 2-3 minutes minimum. Write it ALL down.
   - This is where the real objection lives — you just can't hear it until they've emptied the tank.

3. **Guard drop:** *"Yeah, got it. That's frustrating — makes sense why you'd be cautious."*

4. **Pick the one specific failure and reframe it with logic:**
   > *"Here's the thing — almost every agency that burned a contractor did the same thing: they ran cold ads, generated a list of 'leads,' and shipped the list to 5-10 contractors in the same area. So every lead you got was being pitched by your competitors. You were buying a shared list, not a qualified prospect."*
   >
   > *"We don't do that. 30-mile exclusive radius. Qualification call BEFORE we hand you the lead. $150 only fires AFTER a qualified appointment is booked. So the specific thing that burned you — we structurally can't do it to you. Different model."*

5. **Question-close:** *"With all of that in mind, what do you think is the most intelligent decision from here?"*

### Why this handle works

- **Matt Ryder's reframe is judo.** Instead of defending, you elevate them. They came in defensive; they leave feeling respected AND like a determined operator. That's a completely different buying state.
- Jack: *"Establish trust BEFORE pitch — it's useless to pitch anything unless they believe you can help."* The vent + reframe IS the trust-establishing moment.
- The specific reframe ("that's a structural problem with their model, we don't do that") is what Matt calls a "logical reframe" — better than a metaphor reframe when the prospect is sophisticated.

### What NOT to say

- ❌ *"Oh, those other guys are scams."* (Looks petty, makes you look desperate.)
- ❌ *"We're totally different, here's our 5 pillars..."* (Jumps to pitch before they feel heard.)
- ❌ *"Trust me, we're not like them."* (Trust can't be claimed — has to be earned.)

## The Rule of 3 — when to stop

If you handle the same objection 3 times and they're still pushing back, **stop**. You're not in sales mode anymore — you're in convince mode, and it's lost.

> *"You know what, it's been a pleasure speaking with you. I'm not going to push any more. If you have any questions, my number's saved as More Jobs — reach out anytime."*

**Push the sale away.** If they pull it back, re-engage. If they don't, they were never going to close and you saved yourself 20 minutes.

## Framework recap — use this on EVERY objection

**Parrot** — repeat their exact words back as a question.
**Clarify** — get underneath the surface objection to the real one.
**Guard drop** — "Don't even worry about it, most people ask this."
**Isolate** — "Apart from that, is there anything else stopping us?"
**Handle** — logic reframe (preferred) or metaphor reframe. Never defense.
**Question-close** — "With all of that in mind, what's the most intelligent decision?"

If you can run this in your sleep, you handle 80% of real objections. The words in this doc are examples — the FRAMEWORK is what you actually memorize.
