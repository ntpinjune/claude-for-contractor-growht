## Frameworks & Principles

- **Dream Point over Pain Point** — You don't need a pain point to sell. Create a gap between where they are ($1M) and where they want to be ($3M+) and anchor the service as the bridge.
- **Objection Handling Loop** — Step 1: clarify the objection (don't accept "think about it" — ask what's really behind it: price, trust, logistics). Step 2: isolate it. Step 3: loop back with analogies, firm pokes, humor, interruptions. Guide toward the close by asking questions, not stating.
- **Match-and-Double Energy** — Cocky/A-type prospects need cocky responses. Scared prospects get softer tone, lower volume. Same words, different delivery. If you soft-pedal with an A-type, your opinion becomes worthless.
- **G-Check at Start of Call** — If a prospect tries to reorder the call (wants the price immediately), reset the frame: "Should we do this properly and build a plan, or should I throw you a random number?" They're in your world once they join the sales pool.
- **Laughter Disarms** — Laugh at ridiculous objections. Humans hear laughter and their tension drops; it makes the objection feel small.
- **Layer 3 of the Market = Worst** — Don't run educational ads targeting people who aren't solution-aware. Target solution-aware/problem-aware (top layer) with direct-response still-image ads.
- **Pricing Psychology / Negative Cognitive Bias** — Brain perceives certain price endings as "fair." Ladder: $497, $997, $1,700, $2,700, $3,500, $4,500, $5,500, $7,500, $9,500, $11,500, $12,500. Always end in non-round numbers; jumps of ~$500 feel digestible.
- **Starting At vs Less Than** — For high-ticket, always use "Starting at $X" (where X = area's low end + ~$500). For bait-and-switch/broader funnel, use "For less than $X" with a middle-of-range number. Put actual average range on landing page for expectation balance.
- **Zip-Code Income Targeting** — Pull average household income per zip code in client's service area. Exclude low-income zips. Meta then learns and pushes the ad to high-income people, which kills sales objections and raises close rate.
- **Niche Cap Rule** — Revenue caps when (a) you run out of people to sell to, or (b) low demand. Don't sub-niche when you're outbounding via SMS — construction list burns through in ~2 months at volume. Stay broad within contractor trades.
- **Dopamine Reset (side-tangent)** — Forgetting questions on calls = over-stimulated baseline. Cut artificial dopamine (TikTok, sugar, energy drinks, games). Replace with biscuit, cold shower, sunlight, outdoors, gym. Compounds over weeks.
- **VSL Works Across Niches** — Build testimonial/VSL assets as "for contractors" broadly so they work for any sub-niche pivot without rebuilds.

## Tactical Advice Given

### Ads — Creative & Campaign Structure
- Still-image direct-response ads beat UGC for contractor niches — they target the top market layer (solution-aware).
- Structure: one ad set with student's own creatives (2 creatives) + one ad set with Jack's proven creatives (3 creatives). ~33/66 spend split; heavier on proven.
- Standard per-ad-set mix: 2 images + 1 video.
- If you change niche/product materially, duplicate the campaign — don't reuse old learning data, it's trained on the wrong audience.
- When reviewing creatives: if nothing has spent past target CPL yet, it's still normal learning — don't make decisions early.
- Kill a creative fast only when CTR is ~0.5% (half a percent is dreadful).
- CTR benchmark: 1–2% minimum on link-click-through.
- Cost per booked appointment benchmark: ~$40–$60.
- Target CPL Harry was quoting: $12–$27 on printing ads.
- Concrete sealing price test: "For less than $600" vs "Starting at $497/$497." Landing page should show average $500–$1,500.
- Always specify "whole house window replacement" not just "window replacement" — prevents price-per-pane confusion.

### Metrics Glossary (Jack's masterclass to Harry)
- **CPM** — cost to place ad in front of 1,000 people.
- **Link clicks** — clicks on the link itself.
- **CPC** — cost per link click.
- **CTR (link click-through)** — % who see ad and click the link.
- **Clicks all** — total clicks on ad, comments, profile, or link.
- **CTR all** — % who see ad and click any of those.

### Sales — Closing & Setting
- Setting script is simple: KYC/discovery upfront ("how long you been doing this, what have you tried"), save future-pacing for the sales call, pre-handle objections so they never come up.
- Playing Jack's VSL during sales calls is working for multiple students — Gabriel closed 2, Harry closed 3 today.
- Putting "$100 per appointment" (or price) up front in setter calls drops booking rate but raises show rate and close rate. Harry: ~80% close when prospects show.
- Women make the best setters: prospects don't hang up on them; soft voice hooks them; they imply they'll be "on the call" for higher show rates.
- Bait-and-switch SMS: send it out of the gate. Short, direct, ambiguous ("is it a customer? contractor? subcontracting?"). Longer messages = 3x the SMS cost and less effective.

### Setter/Cold-Call Team Economics
- $100/day per setter, 15 setters = $1,500/day cost -> ~10 appointments/day -> 2–3 pitches/day after show-rate loss -> ~50 good pitches/month -> 20% close = 10 clients/month.
- Setters vs cold-callers: nearly same job. Setters usually work inbound SMS/ads; cold-callers work a list (e.g., Outscraper). SMS slightly more efficient per dollar.
- Average pay-per-lead charge for construction: **$55–$75**. Restoration: stay at $75/lead (don't chase the high end).

### Pricing Benchmarks
- Bathroom remodel starting price (printing): $7,500 ("INO copy").
- High-end California bathroom client: $4,700 cost per lead is fine in rich zips; $24 CPL achievable.
- Concrete estimate cost: $150 when [[business|agency]] pays [[ad spend]].
- HVAC in Florida/Mississippi: $5,500 starting (research via [[ChatGPT]]: "average low and high for HVAC in [area]" -> take the low + $500).
- Window replacement Madison: $9,500 works.
- Epoxy Huntsville AL: $2,800 range.
- Concrete sealing: $497 or <$600.

### Client Operations
- Never let clients handle their own ad spend card if you can help it — bank blocks happen on basically every client. Build ad spend into weekly retainer and pay it yourself.
- When client ghosts: email + text (two touchpoints), not just phone. Frame re-contact as benefit to them. Pause ads as leverage if they refuse to communicate.
- Force clients to use the app ([[GoHighLevel]]) for lead comms — otherwise you have no visibility on what's working.
- Stripe auto-charge workflow for pay-per-appointment: trigger on appointment-booked -> GoHighLevel has native Stripe one-time charge action -> pulls customer ID / payment method ID from Stripe customer record. $1 initial charge captures the card.
