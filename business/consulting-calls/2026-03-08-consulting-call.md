## Frameworks & Principles (the reusable teachings)
- **Lean Mean Machine (LMM)** — the default high-volume video ad script template. Opens with price anchoring ("Epoxy floors starting at $X"). When launching a fresh campaign, always start with the regular LMM script, not a variant.
- **Price anchoring is non-negotiable** — every campaign that has worked historically uses it. If a client complains that the advertised price wasn't honored, that's a sales objection for THEM to handle ("prices do start at $2,500, but for your bathroom at that price it would look terrible"). Don't cave and remove price from ads.
- **The 25%-below-low-end pricing rule** — ask [[ChatGPT]] (or equivalent) for average low/high price for the service in the local area. Take the low end, reduce by 25%, put that in the ad. Example: tool said epoxy low was $2,000 → ad price = $2,500 (slightly above because rounded; the principle is low-end-minus-25%).
- **Reduce factors before launch / one change at a time** — "Simple scales, complex fails." Never change more than one variable at once. If you change landing page AND survey AND creatives at the same time, you can't know what's broken. Start with the proven template; only change things AFTER data proves they're the problem.
- **$90 spend threshold before making decisions** — generally $90 per ad before you judge it, assuming other metrics (CPM, CTR) aren't out of whack. If metrics are bad, you can decide sooner.
- **$20 minimum ad set budget** — ad sets under $20/day will never exit the learning phase. If budget is tight, consolidate all creatives into a single ad set instead of splitting across many.
- **Learning data lives at the ad-set level, not campaign level** — so relaunching "same campaign, new ad sets" gives no benefit over a totally fresh campaign. Pixel/ad-account learning is separate again — more spend on an ad account = better ad account regardless of pixel.
- **I Know copy** — Jack's current best-performing primary-text copy for service delivery ads. Always use it. It can "change the trajectory of results."
- **Square (1:1) image ratio** — Meta pushes 1:1 to more people for less money than 3:4 or other ratios.
- **Captions must stay out of the bottom of the screen** — they get cut off on full-screen playback. Keep text mid-screen where eyes naturally go.
- **Numbers must appear as digits, not words** — "$7,500" not "seven thousand five hundred." Words create mental friction; the brain has to convert.
- **Multi-advertiser ads OFF** — always uncheck it in ad setup. Untick and publish; it doesn't reset learning.
- **Sticky contact ON (in the form settings in GHL, not calendar)** — fixes the bug where appointments come in with only a phone number. It's default-on in Forms → Appointment Booking Form settings.
- **Never explain yourself to clients** — "they paid us for a reason." Tell them what you're doing and why it works. Handle objections, don't defer to their marketing opinions.
- **Don't "cook" for a big client with a custom landing page** — the instinct to jazz it up for a premium client is how you shoot yourself in the foot. Use the proven standard template first.
- **Meta partially resets learning when you replace the landing page** — because the pixel re-scans the page. Change the survey first; only change the full landing page if data forces it.
- **Hook engineering** — the first second has to create "what the hell is this?" curiosity. Jack's go-tos: attractive woman walking across driveway, kid kicking a ball over the fence, dog sprinting out, mom dropping groceries, dude getting hit by a bird, worker about to fall off a roof. Anything that interrupts pattern.
- **Multi-stage closer interview process** — 3+ calls for closers (vs. setters). Each call includes a task; next call evaluates the test. Contract on call 4, then multiple training calls before they touch ads.
- **Don't get greedy with winning campaigns** — if a client has a working $30 CPL and historically pushing down caused problems, leave it alone until the CPL actually drifts up.

## Tactical Advice Given

### Epoxy / luca900
- Relaunch setter ads with calendar as the ONLY thing on the page (white background, calendar slap-bang in the middle). CTA was wrong ("video interview" button scared people off mobile).
- For epoxy client: put "$2,500 starting" in both video and image ads (low end $2k + 25% anchor logic, but rounded up since he charges ~$4k/driveway).
- Switch to standard LMM script (not the variant he used). Untick multi-advertiser ads. Add a price-sign hand-holder image in one quadrant of the 4-image ad.
- Don't relaunch the live campaign yet — let it run, it hasn't spent enough for decisions.

### Driveway/patio / Noah Rascon
- Video said $7,000, copy said $5,500 — fix the mismatch.
- Replace word-numbers with digit-numbers in captions.
- Drop price from "$7,500" to "$5,500 starting" (competitors undercutting). On landing page, raise the average range ($7k-$12k) to filter quality.
- Switch to the I Know copy, standard LMM video scripts — stop using his custom copy.
- Better hooks: attractive woman walking across driveway, mom dropping groceries, burglar in background, dog sprinting out.
- Scrap all current ads, rebuild, send a Loom to Jack to review every creative before launch.

### Bathroom / Nick Borja
- Landing page: revert the survey to the standard one. Don't replace the whole landing page (pixel reset risk).
- $12,500 starting is fine for New Jersey bathroom (market avg $20-27k per ChatGPT).
- Change "Luxury Bathroom Renovation" → "A new bathroom starting at $12,500." People want a new bathroom, not a luxury renovation; luxury narrows audience.
- Ad should be 1:1 square, not 3:4.
- For the $450 upfront collection in 15 minutes: take card details over the phone and charge via whatever payment processor is already live (Blob). Then during onboarding, send a Stripe $1 temp-charge link to set up auto-charge for future. Don't let setup delays kill a sale.
- Contracts: HelloSign or [[GoHighLevel]] both fine.

### HVAC / roofing / Harry Avraam
- HVAC ad hook showing outdoor AC unit = weak (homeowners don't recognize it). Better: happy family turning on AC inside.
- Roofing ad that bombed: replace with Lean Mean Machine format. Use his UK roofing client's winning £7 CPL ad as the template, adapt to US homes.
- Roofing hook idea: start with a worker about to fall off the roof, cut just before impact, then fast 1-second cuts of glamorous finished roofs.
- Georgia roofing: expand targeting from Grovetown to all of Georgia.
- Sticky contact fix: GHL → Sites → Forms → Appointment Booking Form → Settings → Sticky Contact ON (it's in Forms, not Calendar settings).
- Keep the $30 CPL winning campaign alone — don't get greedy by trying to push spend toward cheaper leads.
- For relaunching roofing ads: 2 videos, no image. Different hook on each.

### Tree surgeon reactivation / Sebastians
- Relaunch fresh campaign (new ad sets, new creatives) — don't reuse old ad sets. Drop Christmas-themed creatives. Change creatives completely.
- B2B ad sets at $8.25/day will never exit learning. Consolidate all winning creatives into one ad set at $20+.
- Tree ads: anchor on price in ad, then on landing page state "average tree service ranges $500-$2,000 depending on size" to balance expectations.
- The story-style "helping neighbors" script that was working — keep it since it's producing bookings. Add one LMM-style test ad alongside, not instead.
- SMS launch tags: don't create a new tag per launch. Smart lists filter out anyone who already has the tag. Only contacts without the tag trigger the workflow — re-adding the same tag to the same person WOULD re-blast, so never do that.
- GHL looping workflows: GHL auto-shuts them off after a certain runtime to prevent runaway sends (referencing Nick's 20,000-message disaster).
