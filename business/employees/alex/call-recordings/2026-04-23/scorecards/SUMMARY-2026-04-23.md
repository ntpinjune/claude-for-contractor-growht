## Top 3 coaching priorities for Alex (ranked by revenue impact)

Different ranking than Jowanna's because Alex's failure modes are different. Ranked by what actually costs deals.

### Priority 1 — FIX THE PRICING HE'S PITCHING

**This is critical and training-level, not skill-level.**

On Call 2 Alex pitched: *"For $1000 setup fee you get 15 appointments at $150 per appointment."* And then: *"Our biggest package is $12,000 setup with 300 appointments at $33 per appointment."*

These **do not match your canonical pricing.** Per memory, canonical is: **$1k setup + $150 per booked appointment + $44/day ad spend.** No packaged appointment bundles, no tiered $12k offers.

This is a training drift problem. Alex is using pricing that isn't current. Every prospect he pitched it to now has the wrong expectation. If they come back and accept, the system won't match what they were told.

**Action:** Noah re-trains Alex tonight on the canonical offer. Delete/replace any old training that mentions tiered packages. This is a 5-minute fix that prevents 5 future disputes.

### Priority 2 — STAY IN THE ALIAS, EVEN WHEN ASKED

On Call 1, the prospect asked for Alex's "actual name" — and Alex volunteered "Ali" immediately.

Per script, **Alex Pierce is the persona on every call.** That's who the owner IS from the prospect's perspective. Breaking character mid-call kills the owner frame — same damage Jowanna did when she said "I'll talk to my partners."

**Handle if it comes up again:**
- *"Alex is my name on here, yeah. Anyway — let me ask you about the business..."*
- Redirect within 3 seconds. Don't justify, don't explain the "work name" concept, don't reveal real identity.

**Why this matters:** once the prospect knows Alex Pierce is a persona, they start wondering what else is fake. Trust evaporates. Not a close-rate killer on every call, but a silent deal-loss when it happens.

### Priority 3 — USE THE 20-MINUTE ASK WHEN THE PROSPECT IS WARM

Call 2's prospect was hot. Cody said: *"I think I'll go way easier because I do not want to be looking at that 30-mile radius all day long,"* and *"I'm totally interested in hopping on a call."*

Alex settled for a Monday meeting. That's 4 days from now. 4 days = plenty of time for Cody to cool off, get slammed at work, forget, second-guess, or find a competitor.

**What Alex should have said:**
> *"Cody, honestly — based on what you're telling me, I'm confident we can help. Do you have about 20 minutes right now? I can run you through exactly what we'd do in your area and if it makes sense, we can get you set up today. Otherwise I respect your time and Monday works."*

This is the 20-min ask from the script. It catches same-call closes when the prospect is hot. Losing 20% of hot prospects to a 4-day delay is a real revenue cost.
