### 1. Use his toolkit more aggressively
He has the skills — he's just being too gentle in how he applies them with prospects. Coach him to lean in harder, not pull punches.

### 2. Limited belief: "prospects won't sit there for my objection handling"
He thinks if he probes / handles objections fully, the prospect will bail. This is a head story, not a call reality. Same belief he surfaced on the joint coaching call ("they won't give me enough time of day"). The fix is the same: ask "do you have 20 minutes?" upfront, then if they say yes — they're committed, **run the play in full**.

### 3. Push every call to a real "no" — not a polite close
If a prospect says yes to 20 minutes, that's permission. Don't let them slip out with a soft "let me think about it." Keep pushing objection handling until you get either:
- a sale, or
- a real, firm "no" (the "F you" tier — they're done, period)

A calm, polite close mid-call is the worst outcome. It looks like progress but it's actually him giving up early. The instruction was explicit: **go until "no" means no, not until "no" means "I'm uncomfortable."**

In a previous sales role he had techniques / a toolkit he's not bringing into Contractor Growth — because he wanted to "respect us as business owners" and let us lead. **That's exactly the quality we hired him back for.** We want those past-offer skills running on our calls. Encouragement: bring everything, don't filter.

(Side note: this is why we re-engaged him in the first place — we saw a quality from his past offer we wanted on the team.)
