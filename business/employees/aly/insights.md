_Last updated: 2026-04-24 from the Aly 1-on-1, the Aly+Jowanna coaching call, and the hiking coaching session._

## Strengths (what's already working)

- **Handles objections with composure** — Noah's review of his limited calls flagged this as a clear strength. Nolan also called out that on the trust objection he takes control rather than getting controlled.
- **Comes in with bullets ready** — for "how can I trust you" he fires: 4.8 on Trustpilot + offer of contractor reference + refund clause in the contract. The right shape; he just needs to blend it with Nolan's "nothing's guaranteed but death and taxes" reality framing for prospects who don't actually verify the bullets (most don't).

- **Self-aware about his own gap** — he explicitly named "I need to make a bigger volume of calls" without anyone having to push him there.
- **Has untapped sales skills from a past offer** — the reason we re-engaged him. He's currently holding those skills back because he wants to "respect us as business owners" and let us lead the playbook. Coaching priority: get him to bring all of it.

## Weaknesses / what's killing his deals

- **Loses call control to price questions early** — recent call where prospect kept pushing for price before he could discover, and he gave it. Symptom of no upfront frame-setting ("Do you have 20 minutes? I just want to understand your business first").
- **Has done setting OR closing — not both in one call** — by his own admission. The full-cycle motion is new. Discovery → pain → pitch → close in one go is the muscle he has to build.
- **Can over-explain the offer in detail** — he said himself "I felt like I went through so much of what we're offering in details that I'm like, what do you do on the close call?" Risk: he's pitching everything in discovery, leaving nothing to land the close.
- **Does the math out loud for prospects** — almost said "$44/day = $1,320/month" on a call. Nolan flagged: don't aggregate the number for them. "$44 a day, connect your card" — that's it. Aggregating creates "let me think about it."
- **Limited belief: "prospects won't sit there for full objection handling."** Surfaced both on the joint coaching call ("they won't give me enough time of day") and again on the hike. It's a head story, not a call reality. The fix: ask "do you have 20 minutes?" upfront — once they say yes they're committed.
- **Lets prospects off the hook on a soft "no."** Closes calls polite when he should keep pushing. Coach standard: every call goes until a sale OR a real, firm "no" (the "F you" tier). A calm mid-call close looks like progress but is actually giving up early.

## Coaching priorities (ranked by revenue impact, not ease)

1. **Frame-set at the top of every call.** "Do you have 20 minutes? Are you on a job site?" → if yes-yes, run full discovery. If no, book a real second call with pain established. Without this every other fix is downstream.
2. **Run every call until a real "no."** Once a prospect says yes to 20 minutes, that's permission. Push objection handling to the end. Don't accept polite punts.
3. **Bring the past-offer toolkit in fully.** He has skills he's holding back out of misplaced respect. Encourage him to deploy everything he knows — that's why he's here.
4. **Tighten the discovery → pain → pitch flow.** Use the script structure Nolan walked through: what can we help with → what have you seen that made you feel that way → how long → what have you tried → why not just double down. Probe on negative adjectives ("not enough" → "enough?").
5. **Stop doing the math.** Keep all numbers as daily/per-unit, never aggregated. Setup = $1k, ad spend = $44/day. Period.
6. **Blend bullets + reality.** When prospects don't verify his bullets (4.8 stars, references), pivot to the death-and-taxes honesty frame instead of stacking more bullets.
7. **Volume.** Currently 1,000 SMS/day target. He's right that more reps fix most of this. But fix #1–6 first so volume produces clean data.
