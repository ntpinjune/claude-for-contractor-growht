PART 2: THE 4 OBJECTIONS THAT KILLED EVERY DEAL

Every call died at an objection — not at the opener, not at the pitch. Fix these four and your close rate jumps immediately.

Every handle follows the same framework:

PARROT → GUARD DROP → ISOLATE → HANDLE → QUESTION-CLOSE

OBJECTION 1: "WHAT IF I DON'T CLOSE THE DEAL?"

Where it came up: Call 5.

What the prospect said:

"If I get a lead, I have to pay you for the lead before I even contacted the potential customer or even if I don't close the deal... they wasted $150 of my money."

What you said:

Rambled about trust, reputation, five years of experience. You tried emotion. You didn't give them the math.

What you SHOULD have said:

1. PARROT + CLARIFY: "Totally — when you say 'what if I don't close,' help me understand: what's your current close rate when you meet with a qualified prospect?"

(Listen. Write the number down. This is your ROI data.)

2. GUARD DROP: "Yeah, got it. Don't even worry about it — most people ask this, it's the right question."

3. ISOLATE: "Apart from the 'what if I don't close' piece, is there anything else stopping us?"

4. HANDLE WITH MATH: "Let's do the math. You close around [40%] of qualified prospects. For every 10 leads, you close 4. At $150 per lead, you spend $1,500 to close 4 jobs. What's your average job size? [$15,000]. That's $60,000 revenue from $1,500 spend. Even if you had a terrible week and closed 1 of 10, that's $15,000 from $1,500. How's the math looking?"

5. QUESTION-CLOSE: "With all of that in mind, what do you think is the most intelligent decision from here?"

WHY THIS WORKS: You're not arguing with their fear — you're walking them into a math problem they can't dismiss (because it's THEIR numbers). Once they see the math, the objection disappears.

WHAT NOT TO SAY:

— "Don't worry, our leads are high quality." (They can't verify.)

— "Trust us." (Authority-reducing.)

— "We have 5 years of experience." (Irrelevant to their fear.)

OBJECTION 2: "CAN WE JUST TRY ONE JOB FIRST?"

Where it came up: Call 3 (the closeable Bay Area concrete contractor).

What the prospect said:

"Can we wait until you see if one job works? Let's try one job and see if it works. If it works for me and you, eventually you got to make money and I got to make money, right?"

Why you missed this: This is a BUYING SIGNAL, not an objection. When he said "you got to make money and I got to make money" — that's someone talking themselves INTO a deal. You had to close right there. Instead you restated the $44/day and he walked.

What you SHOULD have said (once our "1 free appointment with setup" offer is live):

"I love that you're thinking this way — it actually lines up with how we're set up. When you pay the setup today, your first qualified appointment is included free. So you literally get to see if it works on the first one. Zero risk on the lead fee. What time works for me to send you the payment link — now or end of day?"

That's assumptive close + risk reversal + same-day urgency all in one.

What you SHOULD have said (without the new offer yet):

1. PARROT: "Try one job first — gotcha. When you say 'one job,' what does that look like? Paying only if you close it, or something else?"

2. GUARD DROP: "Don't even worry about it. Most serious operators ask for some version of this."

3. ISOLATE: "If we lowered your risk on the first one, is there anything else stopping us?"

4. HANDLE WITH REFRAME: "Here's the thing — we're already doing that. The $150 lead fee only happens when we've qualified the lead and booked the appointment. You're not paying for a 'maybe.' You're paying for a qualified prospect showing up. The setup fee gets the whole acquisition system running — first month of ads, qualification system, SEO. Without that, there's nothing to 'try.' But here's what my best clients do: they treat the first 10 appointments as the sample. If 4 close, we're good. If fewer than 2 close, we're doing something wrong and I fix it personally. Fair?"

5. QUESTION-CLOSE: "With all of that in mind, what's the most intelligent decision from here?"

WHY IT WORKS: You reframe "try one job" as "try the first 10 appointments" — flip THEIR frame into YOURS. "I fix it personally" builds trust without reducing authority.

OBJECTION 3: "I WANT % OF SIGNED CONTRACT — NO AD SPEND, NO SETUP"

Where it came up: Call 4 (Alex — your BEST opportunity of the day).

What the prospect said:

"I don't want to pay ad fee, ad spend. I want just flat contract commission. $100,000 project, you get $2,500."

What you said (the WORST moment of your day):

"I can go back to my partners and talk to them about this."

Why this killed the deal: You admitted you're not the decision-maker. Per the script, YOU ARE THE OWNER. The moment you said "talk to my partners," the prospect stopped treating you as the authority. Every closing line after that was dead.

NEVER SAY "I'LL TALK TO MY PARTNERS" in front of a prospect. Ever. If you actually need to check with Noah, reframe as "let me run your specific numbers tonight and come back tomorrow with something tailored." Never break the owner frame.

What you SHOULD have said:

1. PARROT: "You want commission only — 2-2.5% of signed contracts. Got it. Why that specifically? Have you done that with other vendors?"

(Let him vent. Write it down.)

2. GUARD DROP: "I actually get it — it sounds fairer on the surface."

3. ISOLATE: "If I could show you why the flat model protects YOU better, is there anything else stopping us?"

4. HANDLE WITH THE MATH REFRAME:

"Here's why I think flat fees are actually better for YOU, not us. With commission, you pay the same percentage on every job regardless of complexity. A $30k bathroom and a $300k custom home pay us proportionally — $750 on the small one, $7,500 on the big one. But the work to book those appointments is identical on our end. Which means on big jobs, you're OVERPAYING us. And on small jobs, you're paying us for something we haven't really earned.

With flat $150 per qualified booked appointment, it doesn't matter if the job is $30k or $300k — your cost of acquisition is the same tiny percentage either way. On a $300k job, $150 is 0.05%. That's insane value for you.

We're not optimizing for your average job — we're optimizing for your biggest jobs, because that's where your margin is. Does that make sense?"

5. QUESTION-CLOSE: "If the math works — and I think you can see it does — what's the most intelligent decision from here?"

WHY IT WORKS: You flipped the objection. He thinks commission is fairer — you proved it actually robs him on his highest-margin work. You argued his economic interest, not your model.

OBJECTION 4: "I'VE BEEN BURNED BEFORE"

Where it came up: Call 2 AND Call 4. The most common objection of the day — which means this handle alone will change a lot of calls.

What prospects said:

Call 2: "I just came from those guys and those guys told me the same thing that you're telling me."

Call 4: "We've tried what you've told me and we have been burned. 4 months. Zilch nada zero."

What you said:

Jumped to differentiation. "We don't do that. 30-mile exclusive. Qualification system."

Why this failed: When someone has been burned, they need to feel HEARD before they can buy. Jumping to differentiation feels like you're dismissing their pain. They tune out.

What you SHOULD have said (Matt Ryder's reframe — this is the most powerful move):

1. STOP AND REFRAME WITH RESPECT:

"Hold on — you've tried this before? [pause] That actually tells me something really important. It tells me you're someone who doesn't give up, someone who doesn't quit. The fact that you're even on this call after it didn't work out before means you're in the top 1-2-3% of operators who actually want to find a real solution. Most people get burned and just stop trying. Well done."

2. ISOLATE + GET THE REAL STORY: "Walk me through what happened. What specifically didn't work?"

(Let them vent. 2-3 minutes minimum. Write it ALL down.)

3. GUARD DROP: "Yeah, got it. That's frustrating — makes sense why you'd be cautious."

4. HANDLE WITH STRUCTURAL REFRAME: "Here's the thing — almost every agency that burned a contractor did the same thing: they ran cold ads, generated a list of 'leads,' and shipped the list to 5-10 contractors in the same area. Every lead was being pitched by your competitors. You were buying a shared list, not a qualified prospect.

We don't do that. 30-mile exclusive radius. Qualification call BEFORE we hand you the lead. $150 only fires AFTER a qualified appointment is booked. The specific thing that burned you — we structurally can't do to you. Different model."

5. QUESTION-CLOSE: "With all of that in mind, what's the most intelligent decision from here?"

WHY IT WORKS: This is judo. Instead of defending, you ELEVATE them. They came in defensive and wary — they leave feeling respected AND like they're part of an elite 1-2-3%. That's a completely different buying state. Matt Ryder uses this exact move — it works because it's authentic, not manipulative.

WHAT NOT TO SAY:

— "Oh those other guys are scams." (Petty.)

— "We're totally different, here are our 5 pillars..." (Jumps to pitch before they feel heard.)

— "Trust me, we're not like them." (Trust can't be claimed — only earned.)

PART 3: THE RULE OF 3

If you handle the same objection 3 times and they're still pushing back, STOP. You're in convince mode now, and it's lost.

Say: "You know what, it's been a pleasure speaking with you. I'm not going to push any more. If you have any questions, my number's saved as More Jobs — reach out anytime."

Push the sale away. If they pull it back, re-engage. If they don't, they were never going to close.

PART 4: OTHER GAPS WE NOTICED TODAY

Not the top priority (objections are), but fix over time:

1. YOU NEVER ASKED THE 20-MINUTE ASK (0 of 5 calls)

Not once did you ask: "Do you have about 20 minutes? I can run you through exactly what we do and if it makes sense, we can actually get you set up today."

Every call ended with "send me info." Dead prospect in 48 hours. The 20-min ask is the only mechanism for catching same-call closes.

Rule: EVERY call, BEFORE you end it, ask the 20-minute ask. No exceptions.

2. YOU STACK PRICES

"$1,000 setup + $150 per appointment + $44/day for ads" — all in 20 seconds. Prospects said "spicy" and "I don't want to spend $1,000 and find out..."

Fix: Drop ONE clean number first ("$1,000 to get started today"). Pause. Handle temperature check. THEN layer ad spend as a later conversation. Never all at once.

3. YOU DON'T SET THE FRAME (only 1 of 5 calls)

Only Call 5 set the frame. The other 4 went straight from pitch into prospect-driven chaos — they took over, asked for prices, and you lost control.

The frame gives YOU control. Script: "Here's how this goes — I'm going to ask you a few questions about the business. If it looks like a fit, we'll get something booked. You can expect leads as early as next day. This is pretty exclusive — only one contractor per 30-mile radius."

Do this on EVERY call.

4. YOU TALK MORE THAN YOUR PROSPECTS

Script says 70% of KYC should be the prospect talking. You were often 60-70% yourself.

Quick fix: after every question, count to 5 silently before saying anything else. Silence is pressure — they'll fill it.

5. USE THEIR OWN WORDS AGAINST LATER OBJECTIONS

Call 3 prospect said "I want to go full-time on my own business." You should have brought it back: "You said you want to go full-time on your own business. Is $150 per qualified appointment worth it to get there faster?"

Write their exact words down. Use them later. This is how closers close.

6. GO FOR PAYMENT WHEN THEY WARM UP

Call 3 prospect: "If it works for me and you, we can keep doing it." Buying signal. You didn't ask for payment.

Use the Stripe payment link: "I can text you the payment link right now — we can get you started today."

7. HIDDEN FEE DISASTER (Call 5)

At minute 27, after you'd said goodbye: "Oh I'll just tell you one last thing. We do have also a set up fee... $300."

Dropping a fee as the prospect is leaving is the #1 trust-killer in sales. ALL fees get front-loaded at the right time in pricing, never revealed at the end. You probably lost that deal with that sentence.
