## What's actually going wrong (patterns across all 5)

These are not individual mistakes — they're the same mistakes repeating. Fix the patterns and you fix every future call.

### 1. The opener is wrong in every single call (0/5)

The script is: **"Hey, did you see the message my assistant Alice sent over about pay-per-jobs? This is [Your Name]."**

What Jowanna actually says:
- Call 1: "Hi, this is Joanna. I called you this morning." *(no Alice, positions her as cold-caller)*
- Call 2: "Hi, this is Alice. I texted you earlier." *(she said she's Alice — her assistant's name)*
- Call 3: "Hi, this is Alice. I spoke to you earlier today." *(same mistake)*
- Call 4: "Hi, this is Alice." *(same)*
- Call 5: Never names Alice until minute 25 when prospect asks "is your name Alice or Juana?"

**Why this matters:** The Alice framing is what positions her as the BUSINESS OWNER responding to an incoming lead. Without it, every prospect is confused about who they're talking to and whether this is a cold call. One even asked "is your name Alice or Juana?" mid-call.

### 2. She never sets the frame (only 1/5)

The script frame: *"I'm going to ask you a few questions about the business. If it looks like a fit, we'll book you in and I'll walk you through everything. Leads as early as next day. Exclusive — only one contractor per 30-mile radius."*

Only Call 5 set the frame. The other 4 jumped straight from pitch into prospect-driven chaos. **When you don't set the frame, the prospect sets it — and they always set a price-first frame.**

### 3. The 20-minute ask was missed 5 of 5 times

Not once did she ask: *"Do you have about 20 minutes? I can run you through exactly what we do and if it makes sense, we can actually get you set up today."*

**This is the single biggest revenue leak.** The script exists specifically to catch same-day closes. She defaults to "send me info" every time. These prospects were on the phone — ready to move — and she put them in an email queue.

### 4. Price-stacking violates the script (4/5)

Script says: **"Do NOT stack numbers. Present ONE clean number first."**

Jowanna's delivery: "$1000 setup + $150 per booked appointment + $44 a day in ads" all in 30 seconds. Prospects literally said "that's a lot" and "spicy" and "that's a problem, I don't want to spend $1000 and find out..."

**Fix:** Drop ONE number ("to get started, it's $1000 today"). Handle temperature check. Then layer ad spend as a separate, later conversation. Don't fry their brain mid-pitch.

### 5. Objection framework unused in every call (0/5)

Across 5 calls, prospects raised:
- "I've been burned before" (Call 2, Call 4)
- "Can we just try one job first?" (Call 3)
- "I want % of signed contract instead" (Call 4)
- "What if I don't close the deal?" (Call 5)
- "Price is spicy" (Call 3)

Not once did she use **Parrot → Clarify → Guard drop → Isolate → Handle**. She explained every objection away with more pitch content. That's the opposite of the framework.

### 6. Authority collapse on Call 4

The worst moment across all 5 calls: on Call 4, the prospect pushed for a different payment model. Instead of reframing, **she said "I'll go back to my partners and talk to them."**

Per script, **Jowanna is the business owner on this call.** The whole pitch is predicated on her being the decision-maker. Admitting "I need to ask my partners" destroys the authority frame — the prospect now knows she's a sales agent, and every future objection is harder to close.

### 7. The content is actually mostly right

She's delivering the right information in the wrong order, without structure, without the framework that makes it land. **This is a delivery problem, not a knowledge problem — which means it's coachable in 1-2 sessions.**

## Top 3 coaching priorities (ranked by REVENUE IMPACT, not ease of fix)

Every one of the 5 calls this batch died at an objection — not at the opener, not at the frame, not at the pitch. If Jowanna fixed the opener and kept fumbling objections, she'd still close zero. If she fumbled the opener but handled objections with the framework, she'd probably close 1-2 of these 5. So:

### Priority 1 — Objection handling framework (memorized cold)

**Why first:** This is where every call this batch died. Not a maybe — 5 for 5.

Objections Jowanna faced this batch and fumbled:
- "I've been burned before" → Call 2, Call 4
- "Can we just try one job first?" → Call 3
- "I want a % of signed contract instead" → Call 4
- "What if I don't close the deal? Am I paying for leads that ghost me?" → Call 5

She never used **Parrot → Clarify → Guard drop → Isolate → Handle → Question-close**. Explained her way through every one. That's how deals die.

**Action:** She memorizes the framework cold — not as words, as muscle memory. Start with the "been burned" handle since it came up in 3 of 5 calls:

> *"You've been burned before — what specifically happened?" [let them vent, write it down] "Oh got you, got you. Yeah, don't even worry about it. Apart from that, is there anything else that would stop us moving forward? Because here's the thing — the reason they burned you is those agencies send the same leads to 10 contractors. That's a structural problem with their model. We don't share leads. 30-mile exclusive. It's literally a different business model. With all of that in mind, what do you think is the most intelligent decision from here?"*

Drill until she can do it in her sleep. Then layer in the risk handle ("what if I don't close") and the commitment handle ("can we just try one job").

### Priority 2 — Actual close mechanics (the 20-min ask + payment link)

**Why second:** Every hot prospect this batch went to "send me info" and died in an email queue. The 20-min ask is the mechanism to catch same-call closes. She missed it 5 of 5 times.

Worse: even on calls where the prospect warmed up (Call 3 saying "if it works for you and me, we can keep doing it" — that's a buying signal), she didn't ask for payment or a booking slot. She let them walk.

**Action:** Two hard rules on every call —
1. **The 20-minute ask is mandatory.** *"Honestly, based on what you just told me, I'm pretty confident we can help. Do you have about 20 minutes?"*
2. **If they're warming up, go for payment on-call.** *"I'll send the Stripe link right now, we can get you set up today."* The training doc has the payment links ready. She needs to USE them.

### Priority 3 — Authority stance: never "I'll talk to my partners"

**Why third:** Call 4's single worst moment and it killed the best prospect of the day. Per the script, she IS the business owner on the call. The moment she admits she's not the decision-maker, every closing line evaporates — prospects stop treating her as the authority.

**Action:** If a prospect proposes a different deal structure, reframe on the call, don't defer. *"I hear you — most of our best clients have the same instinct. Here's why the per-appointment model actually protects your margin better than a percentage..."* Never "let me talk to my partners" in front of the prospect. If she genuinely needs time, it's *"let me run your specific numbers and come back tomorrow"* — never breaking the owner frame.
