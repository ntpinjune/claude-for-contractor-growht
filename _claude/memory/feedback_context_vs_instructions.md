When Noah describes things about himself (roles, activities, life areas, people in his world), that is **descriptive context for memory**, not an implicit instruction to create folders, files, or infrastructure.

**Why:** On 2026-04-22 Noah said he was 16, in community college, does Stanford research, is in student gov. I over-executed and created `school/`, `stanford/`, `student-gov/` folders in the vault. He corrected me: "I wasn't telling you personally to make a personal journal, school, Stanford, and government. Those are just some things about me." He also said the structure he *would* want is broader (personal/, business/, journal/) with nested subfolders like `insights/` — but explicitly told me not to build it then.

**How to apply:**
- Distinguish context ("I do X") from instruction ("set up Y for X"). Default to saving context to memory only.
- Infrastructure — new folders, files, scripts, scaffolding — requires an explicit ask or a clear problem being solved. "Noah mentioned thing X" is not sufficient.
- When in doubt, ask: "Want me to create a folder/file for this, or just remember it?" Cheap question, avoids unwanted churn in the vault.
- Vault structure should stay broad and general (coarse top-level buckets), not finely sliced by every life area Noah mentions in passing.
