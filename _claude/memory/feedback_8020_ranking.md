When reviewing SDR calls and proposing coaching priorities, rank by **what actually determines whether a deal closes** — not by what's easiest to fix, most visible, or most frequent.

**Why:** On 2026-04-23 Claude reviewed 5 of Jowanna's calls and ranked "fix the script opener" as Priority 1 because it was wrong in 5/5 calls. Noah pushed back: the opener is cosmetic. Every one of those 5 calls actually died at an objection — "been burned," "just try one job," "I want 2% commission," "what if I don't close." Fix the opener and she still closes zero. Handle objections with the framework and she probably closes 1-2 of the same 5 calls.

**How to apply:**
- Before ranking priorities, ask: *at what moment did each call actually die?* Lost deals happen at specific inflection points — identify them, then rank coaching by which inflection point blocked the most revenue.
- "Easy fixes" and "frequent mistakes" are not the same as "revenue-moving fixes." Cosmetic details (openers, wording, tone) rarely determine close rate. Objection handling, close mechanics, and authority framing usually do.
- If a metric says "she got X wrong 5/5 times" — check whether X actually determined the outcome, or whether the outcome was determined downstream of X. A consistent but non-load-bearing mistake is not the 80/20.
- Cross-check rankings against Jack's lens: what would Jack say drives close rate? Almost always objection handling, frame control, and assumptive-close mechanics — not opening lines.
